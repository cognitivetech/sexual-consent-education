## Body Positivity and Compassion

This program is designed to ensure all bodies are more able to meet their needs in a safe and connected manner. In keeping with this purpose is to promote body positivity. The more we are able to find joy in and appreciate our diversity of appearance and ability, we all have a greater ability to meet our own and eachother's needs.

- Encourage appreciation for diverse body types and challenge the overvaluation of conventional attractiveness.
- Foster compassion for oneself and others, recognizing that everyone’s journey is unique and valid.

## Black Culture and Body Positivity

- **Roots in Black Activism:**
    - The modern body positivity movement was founded by fat, queer Black women in the 1960s and 1970s, aiming to create space for marginalized bodies and demand respect from society.
    - This movement was revolutionary, challenging anti-fat and racist beauty norms that had devalued Black women’s bodies for centuries.
- **Wholesome Alternatives to Mainstream Standards:**
    - Black culture has often celebrated fuller, curvier, and more diverse body types as ideal, in contrast to the narrow, Eurocentric standards promoted by marketing firms.
    - Many Black women have developed their own beauty ideals, valuing health, strength, and self-expression over thinness or conventional attractiveness.
- **Cultural Pride and Self-Determination:**
    - Black women have led efforts to promote size inclusivity and self-love, resisting external pressures to conform to unrealistic standards.
    - This cultural approach fosters community, resilience, and a sense of belonging that is often missing from mainstream, commercial body positivity.
- **Challenging Implicit Biases:**
    - Help participants recognize and address their own biases about appearance, gender, and ability.
    - Use exercises and discussions to explore how these biases affect relationships and self-esteem.

## Importance of Respecting Diversity
- **Inclusivity and Empathy:**
    - Respecting diversity means valuing each person’s unique background, identity, and perspective. This inclusivity helps everyone feel seen and valued, which is foundational for trust and collaboration.
    - Empathy grows when we acknowledge and honor differences, leading to richer, more creative interactions.
- **Cultural Openness:**
    - Being open-minded and curious about cultural differences encourages learning and reduces misunderstandings.
    - Diverse perspectives help groups solve problems more effectively and innovate.

## Sexy Is Attitude and Presentation
Sexy is about your attitude, confidence, and how you present yourself to the world—not the shape or size of your body. By focusing on authenticity, self-expression, and positive energy, anyone can feel and be seen as attractive. This perspective challenges restrictive beauty standards and promotes a more inclusive, empowering vision of sexiness.

## Notable Women Authors on Body Positivity
- **Sonya Renee Taylor**
    - **Key Work:** *The Body Is Not an Apology: The Power of Radical Self-Love*
    - **Focus:** Encourages radical self-love and dismantling body shame, especially for marginalized groups.
- **Virgie Tovar**
    - **Key Works:** *You Have the Right to Remain Fat*, *The Self-Love Revolution: Radical Body Positivity for Girls of Color*
    - **Focus:** Combats fatphobia and diet culture, and empowers women of color to embrace their bodies.
- **Megan Jayne Crabbe**
    - **Key Work:** *Body Positive Power: How to Stop Dieting, Make Peace with Your Body, and Live*
    - **Focus:** Shares a personal journey from eating disorders to self-acceptance and advocates for loving your body as it is.
- **Jes Baker**
    - **Key Work:** *Things No One Will Tell Fat Girls: A Handbook for Unapologetic Living*
    - **Focus:** Empowers women to be proud of their bodies and challenges societal fat-shaming.
- **Rebecca Scritchfield**
    - **Key Work:** *Body Kindness: Transform Your Health from the Inside Out – and Never Say Diet Again*
    - **Focus:** Promotes health through self-compassion and body acceptance, not restrictive dieting.
- **Caroline Dooner**
    - **Key Work:** *The F*ck It Diet: Eating Should Be Easy*
    - **Focus:** Critiques diet culture and encourages a healthy relationship with food and body.
- **Kelsey Blackwell**
    - **Key Work:** *Decolonizing the Body*
    - **Focus:** Addresses body trauma for women of color and offers healing practices.
- **Kelly Jensen**
    - **Key Work:** *Body Talk: 37 Voices Explore Our Radical Anatomy*
    - **Focus:** Anthology of diverse voices sharing personal body experiences.
- **E.E. Charlton-Trujillo**
    - **Key Work:** *Fat Angie*
    - **Focus:** YA fiction that centers a plus-size protagonist and themes of self-acceptance.
