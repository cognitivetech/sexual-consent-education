## Female Creators in "High Value Dating"

Some female influencers, like Nelly Sudri (@ask.nelly) and SheraSeven, have gained popularity for teaching women how to present themselves and set standards in dating. While their advice sometimes leans into traditional gender roles and may be controversial, they generally focus on empowerment, self-worth, and boundary-setting—though some critics argue that their tactics can mirror the manipulation seen in male pickup culture.

## Relationship Contracts

A **relationship contract** is a written agreement between partners that outlines expectations, boundaries, commitments, and shared goals for their relationship. While not legally binding in most personal contexts, it serves as a tool for open communication, clarity, and mutual understanding.

### Typical Items Detailed in Relationship Contracts

- **Introduction and Purpose**
    - Names of both partners and the date the contract begins
    - Statement of intent: why the contract is being created and what the couple hopes to achieve
- **Relationship Structure and Exclusivity**
    - Type of relationship: exclusive, open, polyamorous, etc.
    - Rules about emotional or sexual fidelity, and what is allowed outside the relationship
- **Boundaries and Expectations**
    - Emotional, physical, and digital boundaries
    - Expectations for communication, time spent together, and personal space
- **Conflict Resolution**
    - Strategies for handling disagreements
    - Agreement to never threaten the relationship during arguments, or to always address issues before going to bed angry
- **Personal Growth and Self-Care**
    - Commitment to personal development, self-care, and supporting each other’s growth
    - Time for individual hobbies, friendships, and self-reflection
- **Date Nights and Quality Time**
    - Frequency and planning of date nights
    - How often partners will spend quality time together or apart
- **Responsibilities and Household Chores**
    - Division of domestic tasks and responsibilities
    - Who is responsible for what in daily life
- **Financial Arrangements**
    - Handling of shared expenses, savings, or debts
    - Guidelines for spending and budgeting
- **Celebrations and Milestones**
    - How birthdays, anniversaries, and other important dates will be celebrated
- **Transparency and Honesty**
    - Agreement to avoid keeping secrets and to communicate openly
- **Review and Revision**
    - Schedule for reviewing and updating the contract
    - Process for renegotiating terms if circumstances change

## Structure Example

1. **Introduction:** Names, date, and purpose of the contract.
2. **Relationship Structure:** Exclusivity/openness, boundaries, and expectations.
3. **Conflict Resolution:** How to handle disagreements.
4. **Personal Growth:** Self-care, individual time, and support.
5. **Quality Time:** Date nights and shared activities.
6. **Responsibilities:** Household and financial duties.
7. **Celebrations:** How to mark important events.
8. **Transparency:** Honesty and communication.
9. **Review:** Process for revisiting and revising the contract.

## Curriculum: Learning to make people laugh

### **1. Foundations of Humor**

- **Understanding What Makes Things Funny**
    - Incongruity, surprise, and the unexpected
    - Social norms and violations (but not harmful ones)
    - Relatability and shared experience
- **Styles of Comedy**
    - Stand-up, sketch, improv, satire, parody, observational, self-deprecating
    - Choosing and developing your own style

### **2. Mechanics of Comedy Writing**

- **Setup, Punchline, and Tag**
    - Crafting clear, concise setups
    - Delivering punchlines with surprise and timing
    - Adding tags for extra laughs
- **Show, Don’t Tell**
    - Using visuals, actions, and strong verbs
    - Making feelings and attitudes clear through performance or writing

### **3. Structure and Timing**

- **Story Structure in Comedy**
    - Three-act structure: turning point, midpoint, crunch point
    - Sketch structure: exposition, introduce the joke, heighten, punch, button
- **Rhythm and Pacing**
    - Knowing when to pause and when to speed up
    - Building to the laugh

### **4. Character and Conflict**

- **Creating Funny Characters**
    - Exaggeration, quirks, and flaws
    - Characters with different realities or perspectives
- **Comedic Conflict**
    - Setting up opposing forces or misunderstandings
    - Heightening the absurdity


### **5. Practical Exercises**

- **Freewriting and Brainstorming**
    - Exploring topics and finding the funny
- **Writing and Revising Jokes**
    - Cutting unnecessary words, clarifying the premise, strengthening the attitude
- **Workshopping and Feedback**
    - Sharing work in groups, receiving and giving constructive criticism
- **Performance Practice**
    - Trying material in front of live or virtual audiences


### **6. Social and Psychological Factors**

- **Confidence and Vulnerability**
    - Willingness to take risks and be authentic
    - Managing nerves and stage fright
- **Empathy and Audience Awareness**
    - Understanding what your audience finds funny and why
    - Avoiding offensive or alienating material unless you’re skilled at it
- **Cultural Context**
    - Recognizing how background, experiences, and social norms shape humor
    - Adapting material for different audiences


### **7. Special Topics**

- **Improv and Adaptability**
    - Thinking on your feet and responding to the unexpected
- **Collaboration**
    - Working with writing partners and in writers’ rooms
- **Self-Care and Resilience**
    - Handling rejection and learning from failure

### **Relevant Factors Impacting Your Ability to Be Fun and Funny**

- **Personality and Temperament:** Some people are naturally quick-witted or playful; others need to practice.
- **Cultural Background:** What’s funny in one culture may not be in another.
- **Social Skills:** Ability to read the room and adapt your humor.
- **Confidence:** Willingness to take risks and be vulnerable.
- **Practice and Experience:** The more you write and perform, the better you get.
- **Mental and Emotional Health:** Stress, anxiety, or depression can dampen creativity and spontaneity.
- **Audience Dynamics:** Knowing your audience is key to connecting and landing jokes.
