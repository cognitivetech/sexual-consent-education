## Introduction

In regards to sexual etiquette, For most of recorded history, humanity has been taught only, that "no means no," and that rape is forced sexual intercourse. 

Around 2015, as the internet was gaining popularity, the world discovered the concept of "Sexual Consent". According to this, a woman may have the experience of being raped, even if her partner didn't notice any particular signs of distress. 

Because of the "me-too" movement, there is now a public consciousness that women have been experiencing sex as rape, whether their partners realized it or not, throughout recorded history.

## Purpose

I've explored various guidelines related to sexual consent and do not see it as being taught in as comprehensive fashion as I would prefer. 

What's more, many people remove their children from sexual-education in school due to their religious beliefs. Meanwhile, many of our children frequent religious spaces as safe environments that keep them out of trouble. Its concerning to me that these environments may also include children who, due to their parents beliefs, are under-educated on how to relate in a consent-conscious fashion.

Due to the sensitive nature of the subject and sexual taboos, I don't have hope for sexual consent to be taught in a widely effective manner. 

What's more, in my opinion, people who need it the most are men over the age of 30 (who were already in their 20's by 2015). This guide is meant to ensure that at least adults have access to a comprehensive resource for guidance on this crucial matter.

## Compassionate Communication: Needs Based Conflict Resolution

This work is grounded in Marshall Rosenberg's Nonviolent Communication (NVC), promoting the idea that all humans share the same universal needs. NVC proposes that our conflicts arise from our strategies attempting meeting those needs, rather than our needs having a competitive dynamic. Marshall Rosenberg developed Nonviolent Communication to improve the ability for all needs to be met co-operatively, transcending the competitive dynamic where some are met at the expense of others.

A different name for NVC might be *Compassionate Communication*, which helps to highlight why I find that this practice and philosophy ideally suited to discussing the topic of sexual consent. 

##### Nonviolent (Compassionate) Communication Supports Us In:

- A language and philosophy meant to promote the needs of all individuals being met in a given situation.
- Providing empathy for those who have experienced undesired encounters.
- Providing empathy for oneself when our actions have actions been experienced as undesired.
- A mindset of not being attached to any specific strategies for meeting our needs.
- Resolving conflicts surrounding sexual\intimate encounters
- Restorative practices for those wishing to reconcile with themselves and the communities impacted by lack in consent-mindfulness behind our actions
- A broader focus on consent\autonomy in the context of not giving away our power to authority, including a right to choose in any given moment.

These materials are meant to support us, both in individual intimate encounters, romantic relationships, and this challenging transition from an era of "no means no", to a beautiful but complex world where "no doesn't always mean no, and yes doesn't always mean yes". 

**My hope is for this content to promote**: 
- that we aren't unintentionally hurting people we care about
- that our loved ones can go through life unmolested
- a path forward for transgressors to honestly confront their actions and heal relationships in their community and personal lives

## Supporting consent-conscious Communication
Recognizing sex as having profound physical and mental health benefits and as a biological "need" for everyday individuals seeking to maximize their quality of life, while at the same time how profoundly scary it can be for people to even put themselves out there in a world that doesn't fully grasp consent.

To that end, these materials are to include a set of forms where you can describe what consent has looked like for you, various boundaries and romantic interests you have. Then anyone who completes the course can be certified and choose to share their consent story. This practice may promote their ability to engage with others romantically while reasonably assured that they know how to discuss consent. Furthermore, this is in line with the "yes mean yes" policy which requires the initiator of sexual encounters to document efforts to establish consent.

Eventually I would like to transform this into a "dating" site where people can share kinks, boundaries, and consent stories. In that way, this project can help us all have opportunities to connect intimately while ensuring the well-being of all individuals involved.
