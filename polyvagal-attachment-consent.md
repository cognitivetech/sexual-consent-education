# Polyvagal Theory

We've all heard of the "Fight or Flight" response. The idea that when encountering a stressful situation, our bodies Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS) kicks into place, in which our bodies are readied for self-defense. In addition to the popularly known fight or flight responses, there are also the "freeze" and "fawn" responses.

**Freeze** response can be related to *deer-in-the-headlights* behavior, where some predators are less likely to attack when their victim is immobile. (may be an example of Dorsal Vagal Activation)

**Fawn** response could be compared to "Anxious" Attachment, where the individual attempts to pacify an aggressor to avoid violent attack. (may be an example of Sympathetic Activation)

Importantly, its not required for there to be a threat, for this system to be engaged. This understanding is at the core of why Consent became such an important factor to sexual communication.

An example of the freeze response could be a "dead fish" partner who is simply passive, yet non-resisting. The fawn response is demonstrated by a partner who shows all signs of being pleased with the encounter, to prevent any potential of violent assault.

**These automatic responses of the nervous system indicate why _Yes doesn't always mean yes_ when it comes to sexual consent**.

## Attachment theory 
Ideally we want to be in Ventral Vagal state (para-sympathetic), when involved in any social encounter, sexual or otherwise. The fact remains that depending on our personal history and social background, we are commonly in a stress-response. Furthermore, according to John Bowlby's Attachment Theory, our entire personality can be framed by a stress-response rooted in childhood development.

In secure attachment individuals, by default found in the Ventral Vagal state, we automatically reflect the behavior of our peers.

Insecure Attachment individuals will by default trend towards pacifying authority figures by displaying behavior we hope they find favorable (fawn response).

Avoidant Attachment individuals may avoid romantic enounters altogether, or appear to their partner as cold \ distant or non-responsive.
